# Data sovereignty in each track

Data sovereignty concerns Indigenous Peoples' authority in relationships with data and its use. Governance puts that authority into practice through decisions, responsibilities and review. This buildathon treats it as part of framing and doing the work, not only a final warning before publication.

## Three short activities inside project work

| Moment | Student action | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before analysis | Discuss who shapes the question, benefit and authority to consult | Initial non-sensitive decision notes |
| After loading data | Examine representation, missing perspectives and analytical choices | A revised claim or a specific representation limit |
| Before reuse/sharing | Consider a changed audience, options to revise/limit/decline, and corrections | Decision/reason, reviewer roles and a next step |

Each activity takes about 3–5 minutes within group work, followed by a five-minute reuse discussion. Mentors support the conversation; no additional whole-group lecture or switch of tracks is needed. Keep the final 10:45–11:45 sharing hour intact.

## Track emphasis

- Guided Explorer: regional climate divisions versus locally relevant questions; whose priorities shape the interpretation?
- Data Investigator: gauge coverage, weights and comparisons; who decides which measures matter?
- Technical Extender: methodological validity versus authority, and how exports retain both technical lineage and unresolved decisions.

Everyone uses the same provenance structure. Source files are checked against the manifest; each record includes actual analytical settings and the notebook file hash. Student notes use roles and non-sensitive descriptions. Unknown decisions stay unresolved. The optional writer saves classroom drafts only; neither a complete record nor a passed test grants permission.

The record describes the notebook's in-memory results, not a separately exported figure. For a future standalone export, preserve the matching record and add the exported artifact's filename/hash. Notebook hashes identify the saved file, not unsaved cell edits; save the notebook before creating a record you intend to retain.

## Sources and adaptation

Lilly Jones's [Tribal Data Sovereignty Toolkit](https://github.com/daearconsulting/IDSov_toolkit), especially its provenance, adaptation and water-monitoring materials, informed these activities. This repository uses original teaching prompts and a small local helper; it does not copy the toolkit code/templates or require installing its AGPL-licensed package.

[GIDA's CARE principles](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples) emphasize collective benefit, authority, responsibility and ethics. [IEEE 2890-2025](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/2890/10318/) describes a recommended practice for provenance of Indigenous Peoples' data. These are educational references; the exercises do not claim conformance or local adoption. Locally chosen protocols and authorized direction remain essential. FAIR and OCAP® may be discussed using the distinctions in [data governance](data_governance.md), rather than treating frameworks as interchangeable certifications.

## Mentor prompts

Ask what changed in the student's question or claim after considering representation and authority. Invite an unresolved answer rather than a fabricated approver. Distinguish a proposed benefit from a demonstrated benefit, and a person consulted from a person with decision authority. Do not assess how much cultural knowledge a student discloses. Students can use a hypothetical request or decline to discuss protected context.

Assessment remains the seven-question presentation. Look for one concrete governance decision, revised claim or unresolved question with a next step, especially in questions 4–7. Do not add an eighth presentation question or require finished figures.
