# Plain-language glossary

**API:** A structured way for one computer program to request data from another.

**Basemap:** Background map imagery or linework that provides geographic context. It may have its own attribution rules.

**Bounding box:** Four coordinates that form a rectangle around the study area and limit a data request.

**Cache:** A local saved copy that avoids downloading the same information again. Check its creation date before relying on it.

**Coordinate reference system (CRS):** The rules that connect map coordinates to places on Earth. Layers with incompatible CRSs may not line up.

**DataFrame:** A table in Python with named columns and rows, similar to a spreadsheet table.

**GeoDataFrame:** A DataFrame with a geometry column and geographic coordinate information.

**Gauge:** A monitoring station that measures conditions such as streamflow at a specific location.

**PDSI:** Palmer Drought Severity Index, a regional moisture indicator. Negative values are relatively dry and positive values are relatively wet. 

**Proxy:** A measurable substitute for something harder to measure. Roads, for example, do not directly measure community vulnerability, but they can be a proxy for it (along with other data).

**p-value:** Under a statistical model, a measure of how incompatible the observed result is with a no-effect assumption. It is not the probability that a conclusion is true.

**Regression:** A method for describing a relationship or trend between variables. A fitted line does not establish cause.

**Rolling window:** A calculation repeatedly applied to nearby observations, such as each group of seven consecutive days.

**Statistical significance:** A threshold-based statistical judgment. 

**Normalization:** Converting unlike measurements to a common scale. The formula embeds assumptions and should be documented.

**Weight:** A chosen value that controls how strongly one component influences a combined score.

**Uncertainty:** What is not known precisely because of measurement, sampling, modeling, missing data, or interpretation.

**Provenance:** The record of where data came from, when they were accessed, how they were changed, and who is responsible for them.
