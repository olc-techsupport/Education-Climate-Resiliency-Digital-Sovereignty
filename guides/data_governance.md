# Data governance and responsible sharing

This guide is a starting point for discussion, not a substitute for the Oglala Tribe's laws, policies, protocols, departmental review, or community direction.

## Five checks

### 1. Authority

- Who identified the question?
- Who has authority to decide whether the analysis should occur?
- Which Tribal office, community body, or knowledge holder should guide it?

### 2. Provenance

- Who created each dataset, for what purpose, and on what date?
- Does an administrative boundary or federal category misrepresent local geography?
- What transformations and assumptions connect source data to a figure?

### 3. Benefit and risk

- What concrete benefit is expected, and for whom?
- Could maps reveal sensitive locations, resources, practices, or vulnerabilities?
- Could a deficit-focused interpretation stigmatize a place or community?

### 4. Interpretation

- What does community knowledge add, correct, or challenge?
- Are proxy variables being mistaken for lived conditions?
- Are absence and missing data being confused with absence of a problem?

### 5. Sharing and stewardship

- Who reviews the result before it leaves the room?
- What may be shared, with whom, at what resolution, and for how long?
- Where will files live, who can access them, and how will corrections or withdrawal occur?

## Public does not mean permissionless

Federal data may be legally public while analysis or redistribution still carries ethical, cultural, safety, or governance concerns. Do not add community knowledge, interviews, culturally sensitive locations, or unpublished Tribal data to this teaching repository without explicit authorization and an appropriate stewardship plan.

## Framework language

CARE, FAIR, OCAP®, and other frameworks can support learning, but they are not interchangeable and are not evidence that a particular Nation has adopted them. OCAP® is associated with First Nations data governance in Canada. Materials concerning Oglala Lakota lands and waters should prioritize locally selected language and locally authorized governance.

## Output status labels

Use one of these labels on every exported result:

- **Classroom draft: do not distribute**
- **Pending scientific and governance review**
- **Approved for named audience and purpose**

Record approver, date, audience, purpose, source versions, and any expiration or withdrawal conditions. Generated notebook output is a draft by default.

## Minimum provenance record

For each figure or claim, record dataset title, steward, source URL, access date, spatial and temporal coverage, processing steps, missingness, units, key assumptions, code version, intended audience, and review status.


## Complete the practical record

Use the [stewardship template](../templates/stewardship_record.md) and [assessment rubric](alignment_and_assessment.md). Keep completed records in approved class storage. The template covers benefit, source, audience, access/storage, reviewer roles and correction/withdrawal.
