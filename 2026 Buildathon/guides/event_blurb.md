# Data for Sovereignty, Stewardship, and Community Resilience Hackathon

The Data for Sovereignty, Stewardship, and Community Resilience Hackathon at Oglala Lakota College invites students, Tribal staff, faculty, and community members to explore environmental data through the lens of Indigenous data sovereignty. Hosted at Piya Wiconi and led in partnership with ESIIL and Daear Consulting, the hackathon connects practical data skills to questions about Lakota land, climate, and community.

Across two days, each team will develop its own question about topics such as drought resilience, land health, water security, or agricultural stewardship. Participants will choose a supported learning pathway suited to their experience and use prepared local datasets and notebooks to explore their question. Activities are designed to work without internet access during the workshop.

Teams will interpret data, examine uncertainty, and consider what additional evidence would be needed to connect their findings to community decisions. Participants who want an additional technical challenge may build a simple xarray data cube from prepared local data and explore how organizing observations across time and place can support their investigation. This extension is optional; completing a data cube is not required.

Data sovereignty is woven throughout the work: who should guide the use and interpretation of data describing Tribal lands? Who should review the results? What arrangements should govern access, storage, and sharing? Teams will consider these questions alongside their analysis, with attention to local authority and community priorities.

Across the event, participants will:

- Develop a team-defined question and explain why it matters to them.
- Explore prepared environmental data through a supported learning pathway.
- Interpret findings and identify limitations or additional evidence needs.
- Consider potential community benefits and responsible data stewardship.
- Identify appropriate reviewers or collaborators and future project ideas.
- Share what they learned, created, or attempted with peers and community members.

The hackathon builds practical capacity in environmental data science while centering community control over data, decisions, and knowledge.
